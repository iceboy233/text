Fragments of Innocence
2026/08/31

Starlight is distant and cold, yet it comes from stars that have long since vanished. The light people see is merely the lingering echo of a departed world arriving in the present.

Life is ephemeral, the universe is entropic, and meaning perishes as entropy increases.

The true innocence of loveliness is the panic when realizing forever does not exist; it is the honesty of seeing, after the radiance has faded, that one, too, has been running away; it is the admission, before one’s small and desperate fantasies, that one is merely an ordinary person struggling.

Some starlight fades not because the night is too deep, but because they themselves chose to dissolve into stardust.

People will inevitably drift apart, radiance will ultimately depart, and the future may not arrive as it was once pictured. Yet the moment when we shared beneath a clear sky, speaking of the future and laughing, does not become a lie simply because the future has shattered.

It cannot become eternal. But precisely because it is destined to disappear, the wish for it not to disappear grants it a value that transcends eternity.

Though the starlight comes from a past already gone, the fact that souls met in the long night and recognized the light in one another is, in itself, the ultimate act of resistance against nothingness.
